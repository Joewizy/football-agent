# Laws of the Game — selected concepts

## Offside
A player is in an offside position if any part of the head, body, or feet is in
the opponents' half and nearer to the opponents' goal line than both the ball and
the second-last opponent. Being in an offside position is not an offence in itself.
A player is only penalised if, at the moment the ball is played by a team-mate,
they become involved in active play by interfering with play, interfering with an
opponent, or gaining an advantage from being in that position. A player level with
the second-last opponent or level with the last two opponents is not offside.

## Handball
It is an offence if a player deliberately touches the ball with their hand or arm,
including moving the hand or arm toward the ball. It is also an offence if a player
scores directly from their hand or arm, even accidentally, or makes their body
unnaturally bigger by extending the arm above shoulder height. The referee judges
the position of the arm relative to the body movement, not merely whether contact
happened.

## Video Assistant Referee (VAR)
VAR may only intervene in four categories of match-changing decision: goals and
whether an offence occurred in the build-up, penalty decisions, direct red card
incidents, and cases of mistaken identity when the referee cautions the wrong
player. VAR does not re-referee the match; the threshold for intervention is a
clear and obvious error, not a marginal difference of opinion.

## Fouls and misconduct
A direct free kick is awarded for offences such as kicking, tripping, or pushing an
opponent carelessly, recklessly, or with excessive force. A player who denies an
obvious goalscoring opportunity through a foul may be sent off. A second caution in
the same match results in a red card and the team plays the remainder with ten men.
