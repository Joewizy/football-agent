# Competition formats

## Scottish Premiership
The Scottish Premiership contains twelve clubs. Each club plays the others three
times in the first 33 rounds. The league then splits into a top six and a bottom
six based on the standings at that point. After the split, each club plays only the
other five clubs in its half once more, giving 38 matches in total. Points carry
over across the split, so a team cannot overtake a team in the other half on the
final day unless the gap was already small. The bottom club is relegated
automatically and the eleventh-placed club enters a play-off against a
Championship side.

## Danish Superliga
The Danish Superliga uses a similar split system. Twelve clubs play a regular
season of 22 rounds, after which the league divides into a championship round of
six clubs and a relegation round of six clubs. The championship round determines
the title and European qualification places, while the relegation round decides
which clubs drop to the second tier and which enter play-offs.

## Points and tiebreakers
A win is worth three points, a draw one point, and a loss zero. When two clubs
finish level on points, the most common tiebreakers are goal difference, then
goals scored, and then the head-to-head record between the tied clubs. Different
competitions order these tiebreakers differently, so the exact rule depends on the
league's regulations for that season.

## Promotion and relegation
Promotion and relegation link the divisions of a national league system. The
lowest-placed clubs in a division move down at the end of a season and are replaced
by the highest-placed clubs from the division below. Play-offs are often used to
decide the final promotion or relegation place between clubs that finished close
together in the standings.
